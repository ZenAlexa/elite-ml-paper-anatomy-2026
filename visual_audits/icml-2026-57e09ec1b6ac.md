## Figure-by-figure audit

### Figure 1 — p.2

- **Design/grammar:** `scatter`, single-column, one panel. x is log-scaled total model parameters (billions); y is linear average router-collapse level. Light dotted x/y grid, direct model labels, and two legends (bubble-size legend at lower left; model-type legend at lower right). Nine bubbles are visibly labeled although the caption says ten models. Bubble area encodes total size; red/blue encodes reasoning/non-reasoning. Vector chart with an estimated ~0.5 pt frame/grid; one circular marker family, no line series, reference line, hatching or uncertainty.
- **Color/type:** mixed categorical/size key, approximately red `#E76F6A`, blue `#6FA8DC`, gray `#BDBDBD`, black. Color is not grayscale-safe because model type is carried primarily by red/blue.
- **Data/statistics:** layer-averaged collapse level for MoE LLMs against total parameters; model size is a bubble-size encoding. No denominator, layer aggregation detail, exact values, uncertainty, or failure-value convention is given.
- **Caption:** “Figure 1. Average router collapse levels across layers for ten state-of-the-art MoE-based LLMs. The results demonstrate that while all models exhibit router collapse, the intensity varies between reasoning and non-reasoning models. Best viewed in color.” It supplies title, comparison and finding, but not x/bubble/legend definitions; it is not fully self-contained.
- **Evidence relation:** introduction's “all models exhibited router collapse” → Figure 1 establishes the problem and motivates “without a router?” → Figure 3 gives the alternative interface → Figures 6–7 and Tables 5/15 test mechanism and non-copying.
- **Strengths:** parameter scale, collapse level, model type and model size are combined in a compact headline view; labels avoid a separate lookup for the named models.
- **Weaknesses:** label crowding and tiny text; the visible nine-bubble/caption-ten discrepancy; no error or exact-value support; red/blue dependence limits grayscale use.
- **Reusable pattern:** use a headline bubble scatter when scale, a continuous failure measure and one categorical split must be shown together, but state the aggregation and count in the caption.

## Cross-object judgment

The visual narrative is: Figure 1 establishes collapse across model scale; Figure 3 supplies the eigenvector-routing interface; Figure 2 and Tables 1–3 carry performance, memory, robustness and retrieval; Figure 4 adds a semantic/cost view; Figure 5 compresses in-context results; Tables 5–6 test mechanism and hyperparameters; Figures 6–7 provide correlation and latent-structure diagnostics; appendix Tables 7–16 expand modalities, prompts, router alternatives, distribution-sensitive controls and capacity. Captions consistently begin with the object label and usually state a comparison; only some define encodings or uncertainty. Tables use a stable booktabs-like horizontal-rule system, model/task condition blocks and bold best cells. The dominant visual language is Nimbus Roman/Computer Modern for paper text and captions, with DejaVu Sans in chart labels and raster heatmaps; chart colors are categorical and usually method-coded but are not reliably grayscale-safe. The clearest reusable design is the paired control structure: headline/reduced-memory result → full-capacity or expanded-table control → targeted failure/mechanism diagnostic. The main failure modes are small dense labels, undefined error bars/uncertainty, unlegended dual-axis cost lines, color-only encodings, and captions that omit matrix/legend semantics.

**Highest-value objects:** Figure 2 for the headline performance–memory trade-off; Table 1 for the full-capacity/pruning control; Table 3 for cross-modal clean/corrupt retrieval; Figure 6 for the theory-to-mechanism visual link; Tables 13–16 for diagnosing flips, distribution behavior, router complementarity and capacity failure.

**Most reusable patterns:** matched-scale panels with a common benchmark order; hierarchical table headers for condition × method × metric; full-capacity controls next to reduced-cost methods; transition/overlap diagnostics that test alternative explanations; embedding panels paired with explicit quality metrics.

**Failure patterns:** caption-ten versus visibly nine bubbles in Figure 1; Figure 4's secondary-axis lines lack a complete legend; Figure 5 error bars are undefined; Figure 6 color scales and correlation units are under-described; Tables 9–11 repeat a duplicated method name in the caption; many objects depend on color and tiny labels; most results lack uncertainty or repetition denominators.

**One-sentence visual strategy:** The paper uses compact method-coded comparisons and appendix diagnostics to move from collapse observation to eigenvector routing, with strong matched controls but limited source reproducibility and several color/uncertainty semantics left implicit.
