## Paper-wide visual style

All ten visual objects are raster PNG plots embedded in vector LaTeX. The plot interiors use the Matplotlib-like light-gray axes background and black spines/ticks; the surrounding page and captions use Computer Modern. Plot text is a rendered estimate of DejaVu Sans at roughly 5–6 pt after PDF placement; captions are approximately 10 pt Computer Modern Roman, with ordinary-weight label text. The recurring data colors are exact saturated raster pixels `#FF0000`, `#0000FF`, `#008000`, and `#BFBF00`; Figure 1 also contains `#1A3380`. Red dash-dot, blue dashed, green/yellow dash-dot, and occasional dotted lines carry most series identity. No plot has visible grid lines, markers, reference lines, bands, error bars, or other uncertainty displays. Legends are small, boxed, and inside the lower-right of each axes.

## Object-by-object audit

### Figure 1 — p. 9, deterministic valuation sweep

- **Geometry/type:** centered, single-column, one-panel line plot; raster; five trajectories and a five-item lower-right legend. x is linear `Number of Rounds` (0–300,000); y is linear `Time-Average Revenue` (roughly 0–1). No markers, grid, direct labels, or reference line; three line styles (dash-dot, dashed, dotted) distinguish five color-coded series.
- **Encoding:** red dash-dot `v2=0`; yellow dotted `v2=0.2`; blue dashed `v2=0.5`; dark blue dotted `v2=0.8`; green dash-dot `v2=1`. This is a categorical palette with only partial line-style redundancy and is not reliably grayscale-safe.
- **Data/statistics:** deterministic first-price auction, `n=3`, `(v1,v2,v3)=(1,v2,0)`, `k=10`, `v2∈{0,0.2,0.5,0.8,1}`; each curve is a time-average trajectory. The paper states that all cases exceed `v2−0.1`; that threshold is not drawn and no seed, repeat count, uncertainty, denominator beyond the implicit round average, or failure value is reported.
- **Caption:** “Figure 1: Time-averaged revenue for different values of `v2`.” It has title and comparison moves, but no setup, encoding key, or main finding; it is not self-contained.
- **Evidence relation:** p. 1 Question 1.1 (finite horizon) → pp. 5 and 9 Theorem 2.6/Equation (3) → deterministic valuation sweep → this figure → p. 9 claim that trajectories surpass `v2−1/k`. It is empirical main-comparison evidence, not a proof of the universal theorem.
- **Strengths:** shared axes make the five valuation cases immediately comparable; the legend maps each curve to the parameter; rapid initial movement and long-run separation are visible in one compact panel.
- **Weaknesses:** colors carry most of the five-way identity; the claimed `v2−0.1` benchmark is absent; the tiny raster and sparse caption hide `n`, `k`, horizon, and averaging details; a single unreplicated trajectory has no stability information.
- **Reusable pattern:** use one common round/revenue frame for a controlled valuation sweep, but add a visibly labeled theorem threshold, non-color redundancy, and the full setup/replication definition to the caption.

### Figure 2 — p. 10, discretization/convergence trade-off

- **Geometry/type:** centered, single-column, one-panel raster line plot; three trajectories and lower-right legend. x is linear rounds (0–300,000); y is linear revenue (approximately 0.6–1.0). No markers, grid, direct labels, or reference line; two line styles (red/green dash-dot and blue dashed).
- **Encoding:** red dash-dot `k=10`; blue dashed `k=30`; green dash-dot `k=50`. Categorical color is only partly redundant because red and green share the dash-dot style; grayscale-safe reading is poor.
- **Data/statistics:** deterministic `n=3`, valuations `(1,1,0)`, `k∈{10,30,50}`. It shows larger `k` giving a higher apparent long-run revenue but slower approach. There are no repeats, seeds, uncertainty marks, or exact convergence criterion; the stated target `v2` is not a horizontal line.
- **Caption:** “Figure 2: Impact of discretization level `k` on revenue convergence.” It supplies title and a parameter cue only; it omits the valuation profile, number of bidders, horizon, and main comparison result, so it is not self-contained.
- **Evidence relation:** Equation (3) and Corollary 2.7 motivate the `k` dependence (pp. 5 and 9); the p. 9 setup states the controlled `(1,1,0)` experiment; p. 10 Figure 2 supplies the efficiency/convergence comparison and supports the text’s claim that empirical convergence is faster than the theorem rate.
- **Strengths:** a common scale exposes the speed/limit trade-off without panel changes; three simple paths keep the mechanism readable; legend labels are short.
- **Weaknesses:** no theorem/reference baseline or convergence-time marker; red and green collapse in grayscale; caption does not carry the controlled setting or the “higher but slower” finding; no uncertainty or repeated-run evidence.
- **Reusable pattern:** show discretization as a common-axis trajectory comparison, while adding explicit target/reference lines and a predeclared convergence measure.

### Figure 3 — p. 10, binary Bayesian revenue by bidder count

- **Geometry/type:** centered, single-column, one-panel raster line plot; four trajectories and lower-right legend. x is linear rounds (0–100,000); y is linear time-average revenue (0–1). No markers, grid, direct labels, reference line, or uncertainty; two line styles (blue dashed, dash-dot for the other series).
- **Encoding:** blue dashed `n=2`; green dash-dot `n=4`; red dash-dot `n=3`; yellow dash-dot `n=5`. The palette is categorical and mostly color-dependent; it is not grayscale-safe.
- **Data/statistics:** i.i.d. binary valuations `vi∈{0,1}` with probability `1/2`, `k=10`, `n∈{2,3,4,5}`; each line is a time-average revenue trajectory and the intended comparison is `E[v2]=1−(n+1)2^{-n}`. The expected-value baselines are not drawn, and no seed/repeat/error summary is reported. Each bidder uses parallel no-swap learners for the two realised values.
- **Caption:** “Figure 3: Revenue produced for different number of bidders.” It has title and broad comparison moves only; it omits the Bayesian distribution, `k`, axis semantics beyond the plot, expected-value target, and main finding, so it is not self-contained.
- **Evidence relation:** p. 2 Claim 1.7 and p. 9 Section 4 motivate finite-time revenue; p. 9 describes the binary Bayesian setup; p. 10 Figure 3 is qualitative evidence that revenue approaches expected second-highest value. - **Strengths:** one axis lets the reader compare bidder-count-dependent plateaus; legend is compact and the initial transient is visible.
- **Weaknesses:** expected `v2` values are only implicit in text; color is the primary identity channel; the caption omits all Bayesian controls; no replication or uncertainty distinguishes a trajectory from a robust estimate.
- **Reusable pattern:** pair a discrete population-size sweep with one common revenue axis, and draw the analytically computed target for every series.

## Cross-object system

The visual narrative moves from deterministic valuation cases (Figure 1), through discretization efficiency (Figure 2), to binary Bayesian revenue (Figure 3 and its Appendix E repeat, Figure 4), then to conditional Bayes Mixed Nash CDF diagnostics (p. 24–25). Captions use a repeated “quantity for parameter” sentence and rarely carry setup or findings; Appendix E replaces numbered captions with unnumbered brace text. Main/appendix linkage is explicit in p. 10 (“deferred … to Appendix E”), but Figure 4 is a literal duplicate rather than an independent extension. The figures do not form a method-interface or ablation system: Algorithm 1 and the LP/dual-fitting proof are text/formula objects, while plots are downstream empirical evidence. Typography and raster style are consistent across all objects; color semantics are locally consistent (red empirical/no-swap, blue theory where overlaid, red/blue/green/yellow parameter series) but multi-series plots rely too heavily on color and provide no uncertainty encoding.

## Final judgment

- **Most reusable patterns:** common-axis trajectory sweeps for controlled `v2`, `k`, and `n` comparisons; direct theory-versus-empirical CDF overlays with line-style redundancy; a compact appendix panel set for conditional strategies.
- **Highest-value objects:** Figure 1 for the valuation-sweep headline; Figure 2 for the revenue/convergence trade-off; the p. 24 `v1=1` CDF overlay because it directly compares the stated Bayes Mixed Nash target with empirical behavior.
- **Failure patterns:** sparse/non-self-contained captions; no error bars, bands, repetitions, or quantitative CDF discrepancy; absent theorem/expected-value reference lines; color-heavy legends; unnumbered appendix panels; and the p. 25 prose/axis horizon mismatch (`T=50000` versus an x-axis through 500000).
- **One-sentence visual strategy:** use small, consistent raster trajectory and CDF panels to provide qualitative evidence that no-swap-regret revenue and conditional bidding behavior approach their deterministic or Bayes Mixed Nash targets, while leaving the principal theorem and its proof in text and formulas.
