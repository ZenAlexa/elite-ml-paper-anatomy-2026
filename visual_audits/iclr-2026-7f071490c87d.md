## Object-by-object audit

### Main-text figures

#### Figure 1 — p. 7, §5.1, `single_column`

A 2×2 qualitative trajectory grid with panels `(a) K_I`, `(b) K_I − Û`, `(c) K_θ`, `(d) K_θ − Û`. Each panel overlays four condition-specific semicircular trajectories: dense colored true samples at three times, with lighter samples for larger `t`, and thin same-color model paths from `t=0` to `t=1`. The panels share the same 2-D geometric frame and use panel labels rather than a separate legend; faint grid lines are visible. The caption is: “Figure 1: Dots represent true samples from the temporal process across t ∈ [0, 1], lines represent model estimated trajectories from t = 0 to t = 1. Each condition has a distinct colour.”

The object directly binds the method’s two inductive biases—learned metric and density potential—to the semicircle mechanism; Table 1 supplies the withheld-time NLL/CD test. Strengths are matched axes, a compact four-way ablation, and simultaneous visibility of condition crossing and curvature. Weaknesses are the color-only condition key, dense overplotting, no numeric axes or uncertainty definition, and a caption that does not name the four panel variants. Reusable pattern: use a common coordinate frame and matched ablation panels to show how each mechanism changes a geometric path.

#### Figure 2 — p. 8, §5.2.1, `single_column`

A single linear line chart of (P_{NK}) versus (lambda_{NK}) from 0 to 10. Blue circular-marker `True` and red circular-marker `HTI Surrogate` curves are overlaid with translucent same-color bands; the legend is in the upper-right and there is no visible grid. The caption is: “Figure 2: P_NK vs. λ_nk for ground truth policies and our surrogate policy.” The plot shows the surrogate tracking the true curve, with both decreasing from the high value at λ=0 and converging at the upper end.

This is the mechanism-level check paired with Table 2: reward is the decision metric, while Figure 2 checks the NK-preservation trajectory that the reward weight is intended to control. The bands visually communicate run variation, but the caption does not define whether they are SE, SD, or another interval. The short caption also omits the held-out/training split and sample aggregation. Reusable pattern: pair a compact trajectory plot with a scalar outcome table when the scalar metric could hide an important mechanistic trade-off.

#### Figure 3 — p. 9, §5.3, `single_column`

A 4×3 small-multiple grid (12 panels): rows are Direct, CFM, MFM, and Ours; columns are three randomly selected ETTm2 samples. Every panel shares the time-step forecast layout: a thin black history line, a green true central-80% interval, and a method-colored surrogate interval after the forecast boundary. The shared legend is placed in the upper-left panel and lists History, True, Direct, CFM, MFM, and Ours; only bottom-row panels carry x tick labels and only the first column carries y tick labels. The caption is: “Figure 3: Central 80% prediction intervals from HTI surrogates compared with the true intervals on randomly selected ETTm2 samples, for direct (top), CFM (second row), MFM (third row), and our (bottom) approach.”

The grid gives qualitative shape/width evidence for Table 5’s MSE comparison and makes the method-row comparison immediately scannable. It is high information density: small axes, method-colored fills, one shared legend, and no explicit sample IDs or numerical uncertainty definition. The caption defines the interval and row ordering but not the column samples, the forecast horizon, or the meaning of the green band beyond “true intervals.” Reusable pattern: a method-by-instance matrix with shared scales and a single legend is effective for interval-shape comparison, provided the sample selection and interval construction are stated.

### Appendix figure

#### Figure 4 — p. 16, Appendix A, `page_width`, raster conceptual diagram

The figure is a two-part conceptual composition: a large left (\mathbb{R}^d) box with blue and red conditional manifolds (\mathcal{M}_{x_1}), (\mathcal{M}_{x_0}), point clouds, labeled anchor distributions (p_{θ_{λ_0}}(·|x_i)) and (p_{θ_{λ_2}}(·|x_i)), solid blue/red trajectories, and dotted connectors to a right-side user list with λ values 0, 0.1, 0, 0.4, 0.9, 0.6, 0.7, 1.0 plus a brace grouping surrogate outputs. `pdfimages` confirms eight 512×512 RGB tiles with alpha masks; text/caption remain PDF vector objects. The long caption is: “Figure 4: Example inference-time adjustment enabled by HTI. We illustrate disparate user preferences affecting desired NN behaviour (desired λ level) for different users in this abstract example. Having a fixed number of trained NNs (p_{θ_{λ_i}}) only allows partial exploration of the full hyperparameter trajectory, while an HTI surrogate model ((p̂(·|x_i, λ))_{λ∈Λ}) can estimate outputs across the entire spectrum of hyperparameters (estimated conditional probability paths represented by solid blue/red lines). Crucially, hyperparameter-induced dynamics can differ amongst input conditions (x_i), as the true conditional distributions move along their own respective manifolds (M_{x_i}), so an effective HTI model must learn conditional dynamics.”

It functions as the method interface/headline diagram for inference-time adjustment and condition-dependent dynamics, but it appears in Appendix A after the main empirical story. Labels, manifold fills, colored paths, brace, and user icons give redundant conceptual cues; the raster tiles and small formula labels make close inspection/gray-scale reproduction weaker. The caption is unusually self-contained and states the mechanism, yet it is too long for a compact figure and leaves the user λ list abstract. Reusable pattern: show sparse anchors and continuous conditional paths in one conceptual plane, with a separate user-demand lane to connect geometry to inference-time use.

## Cross-object system

- **Visual narrative:** method geometry is introduced through Figure 1’s four-way path ablation and Table 1’s withheld-time metrics; Tables 2–4 move the same surrogate suite into linear/non-linear reward weighting; Table 5/Figure 3 transfer it to quantile intervals; Table 6 and Table 7 close with generative distance and metric parameterization; Figure 5 then tests the sparsity boundary in the appendix. Algorithm 1/2 and Tables 8–15 provide the training/sampling and reproduction support behind those objects.
- **Caption system:** captions consistently give an object title plus a setting and comparison, while Figure 1/3 explicitly decode marks or row order. Table captions usually identify the evaluation grid and metric direction, but standard-error semantics and sample denominators live in §5 rather than each caption. Figure 5 is the weakest caption because it is title-only; Figure 4 is the strongest because it states setup, encoding, and mechanism.
- **Table header system:** compact `Method` plus a directional metric (`↓`/`↑`) dominates Tables 1–6; Table 7 adds grouped task headers and reuses arrows; Tables 8–15 use the stable `Hyperparameter`/`Value` pair. Booktabs rules, centered headers, and no cell colors keep the table family coherent.
- **Method/result/ablation link:** Figure 1/Table 1 isolate the density potential and learned metric; Table 7 isolates eigenvalue parameterization; Tables 2–6 test the resulting full method against direct, CFM, MFM, NLOT, and partial-bias variants. The same object grammar makes the causal chain from mechanism to cross-domain result legible.
- **Main/appendix link:** body objects point to Appendix C for data/model details; Appendix A’s Figure 4 explains the inference-time interface, Appendix B’s Algorithm 2 explains sampling, Tables 8–15 expose hyperparameters, and Appendix E’s Figure 5 bounds the sparsity claim. The appendix is functionally connected rather than a second visual narrative.
- **Typography:** the paper’s LaTeX body, captions, equations, and tables are primarily Nimbus Roman/Computer Modern; plot axes and the raster conceptual diagram introduce DejaVu Sans-like rendering. Main and appendix table typography is stable; Figure 3’s 12 panels and Figure 4’s small labels compress readability.
- **Color:** plots reuse a Matplotlib-like categorical palette (blue/orange/green/red/purple/brown) for methods and true/model bands; Figure 1 uses color for conditions, Figure 2 uses blue/red for true/surrogate, and Figure 4 uses blue/red manifolds. The palette is semantically useful but not grayscale-safe, and most method colors lack a distinct marker or line-style redundancy.

## Final judgment

- **Most reusable patterns:** matched mechanism-ablation panels plus a withheld-time table; one-metric result tables with explicit directional arrows and held-out grids; method×sample interval small multiples with shared scales; common task panels for anchor-sparsity stress tests; stable booktabs `Method`/metric and `Hyperparameter`/`Value` grammars.
- **Highest-value objects:** Figure 1 + Table 1 (mechanism closure), Figure 3 + Table 5 (shape and aggregate forecast evidence), Table 7 (cross-task parameterization ablation), and Figure 5 (claim boundary under sparsity).
- **Failure patterns:** captions often defer SE/band definitions and sample denominators to body text; Figure 5 has no finding or uncertainty statement; color carries most method/condition identity and degrades in grayscale; Figure 3 and Figure 4 are label-dense; exact target figure/table source files are not present in the public author repository.
- **One-sentence visual strategy:** use compact geometry and interval visuals to make conditional trajectories inspectable, then repeat a disciplined one-metric table grammar across application settings and place the reproducibility/sparsity boundary in the appendix.
