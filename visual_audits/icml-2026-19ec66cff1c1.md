## Cross-object system

- **visual_narrative:** The visual sequence moves from the AXP headline frontier (Figure 1), calibration (Figure 2), one dynamics illustration (Figure 3), and replicated summary (Figure 4), then expands into diagnostics, local-adaptivity panels, distributional Pareto slices, tables, and explicit failure cases (Figures 78–80).

## Final judgment

- **Most reusable patterns:**
  - A shared size–coverage objective-space grammar across mean, median, 75% quantile, and calibration views.
  - A four-panel local-adaptivity template pairing rolling coverage, interval size, and raw prediction sets for one baseline at a time.
  - A compact booktabs table combining validity, error-run length where available, central size, and upper-tail size while preserving ∞ failures.
  - Explicit failure captions (Figures 78–80) that connect a visual symptom to a controller/learning-rate mechanism.
- **Highest-value objects:**
  - Figure 1: the clearest headline size–coverage trade-off for AXP.
  - Figure 4: replicated synthetic comparison with SEM bars.
  - Table 1: compact validity, run-length, and tail-size decision surface.
  - Figures 78–80: high-value negative examples that make failure boundaries concrete.
- **Failure patterns:**
  - Repeated color-only method encoding and small legends reduce grayscale/accessibility robustness.
  - “As in Figure …” captions omit panel semantics and target definitions when figures are read independently.
  - Figure 3 caption calls a time-series tracking display a Pareto frontier.
  - Appendix synthetic tables omit the longest-error row, and captions generally omit uncertainty/aggregation details.
- **One-sentence visual strategy:** Use a stable red-UP-OCP versus categorical-baseline grammar to move from one AXP headline frontier to repeated local, distributional, calibration, and failure views that test validity and set-size tails across datasets.
