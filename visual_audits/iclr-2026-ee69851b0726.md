## Paper-level visual style

- **Figure typography:** body text and captions use embedded Nimbus Roman No9 L with Computer Modern math. The plotted raster assets use a compact sans-serif plotting face (visually consistent with DejaVu Sans/Arial-like labels), while Figure 1 mixes native PDF/math text with diagram labels. Across the figures, plot text is approximately 6–10 pt at the placed size; the conceptual Figure 1 has labels spanning about 8–12 pt. Exact PDF caption text is about 9.963 pt (`pdftohtml -xml`, `pdf_object`, high confidence); plot label sizes are rendered estimates.
- **Table typography:** Tables 1–10 are native text/vector tables using Nimbus Roman No9 L and Computer Modern math glyphs. Body text is approximately 8.5–9 pt and bold headers approximately 9.5–10 pt; `pdffonts` confirms the embedded Roman/math families. Table captions are compact regular serif text, while parameter headers are bold.
- **Palette strategy:** FIRE is consistently red in the principal visual comparisons. Vision figures add yellow/orange/pink/purple/green/teal/blue/gray method colors; LLM and RL panels use local subsets of that categorical palette. Reference lines are black/gray dashed. The palette is expressive but not designed for grayscale or color-vision-deficient reproduction because several neighboring methods differ mainly by hue.
- **Vector/raster mix:** Figure 1 is primarily vector PDF diagram geometry/text. Figures 2–8 are embedded raster composite plots (with soft masks), while Tables 1–10 are native text/vector. The raster XObjects are high-resolution within the PDF (roughly 600–840 ppi at the placed dimensions), but their plotting source and data are not published in the inspected repository.
- **Style consistency:** the paper's surrounding ICLR serif/caption/booktabs system is stable. The imported plots share white backgrounds, light gray grids, compact sans-serif labels, and red FIRE emphasis, but panel-specific legends and method colors are local. The appendix settings tables are typographically consistent with the manuscript but function as configuration inventories rather than result tables.

## Cross-object visual system

- **Visual narrative:** Figure 1 establishes the constrained stability–plasticity geometry; Figure 2 carries the main visual-learning protocols; Figure 3 tests checkpoint sensitivity in LLM continual pretraining; Figure 4 extends the intervention to discrete and continuous RL; Figure 5 separates iteration choice from curvature/DfI/SFE proxies. Appendix Figures 6–8 explain iteration dynamics, NS coefficient robustness and train-side recovery. Table 1 supplies compute overhead, Tables 2–5 fix algorithm settings, Table 6 records search spaces, and Tables 7–10 record selected values.
- **Caption system:** all objects use `Figure/Table n:` captions with a descriptive title. Figure captions generally bold the title phrase and then describe setup/comparison; Figures 2–4 define the protocol or intervention marker, but none defines shaded-band semantics. Figure 5's error bars are undefined, and Figures 6–8 omit scale/uncertainty details. Table captions are short title-only labels, with hardware, seed count, schedules and parameter semantics often left to surrounding prose.
- **Table-header system:** parameter tables use stable `Parameter | Value`, while search/selection tables use `Experiment | Method | Hyperparameters | Search Space` or `Dataset | Method | Value`; Table 10 adds `CKPT`. This is easy to scan but the generic Value column hides which hyperparameter a number denotes in Tables 7–10. No result table combines performance, cost, failure and uncertainty.
- **Method/result/ablation link:** the theory defines SFE as stability and DfI as plasticity; Figure 5(b) visualizes the proxy separation, Figures 2–4 show downstream behavior, and Figure 6 visualizes the iterative approach to the target. Figure 7 tests the coefficient choice, while Figure 8 gives training dynamics behind test curves. The relation is conceptually coherent, but rank/dormancy consequences from the theory are not directly measured and the proxy bars have no numeric companion table.
- **Main/appendix link:** main figures carry the conceptual, cross-domain and ablation storyline. Appendix B adds cost, iteration, coefficient and train-accuracy diagnostics; Appendix C gives implementation detail; Appendix D describes baselines; Appendix E gives seeds, hyperparameters, search ranges and selected settings. The appendix extends the same claim families rather than introducing another chart grammar.
- **Typography consistency:** native manuscript/caption/table typography is highly consistent (Nimbus Roman/math). Raster plot labels are compact and broadly consistent across Figures 2–8, while Figure 1 is a separate diagram style. At 200 dpi the panels are legible, but 6–8 pt plot legends and eleven-method palettes are small.

Figure 2 and Figure 8 reuse the vision palette, but Figure 3 and Figure 4 have local legends. The system is not grayscale-safe and no table encoding carries the color semantics.

## Final judgment

### Most reusable patterns

1. A compact conceptual geometry diagram that maps an optimization constraint to the intersection of competing properties.
2. Protocol-aligned small multiples with shared axes and a common method legend for warm-start, continual and class-incremental regimes.
3. A mechanism-oriented ablation that places iteration cost, curvature/plasticity/stability proxies and a full-reset reference side by side.
4. Appendix configuration tables that separate defaults, search spaces and selected per-dataset/checkpoint values rather than mixing them with outcome tables.
5. Intervention-aligned RL facets with a shared vertical event marker across tasks and control families.

### Highest-value objects

- **Figure 2:** the main visual-learning evidence surface; its row structure exposes protocol differences and its test curves connect directly to the stability–plasticity claim.
- **Figure 3:** the strongest checkpoint-sensitivity view for continual LLM pretraining, because the three initialization conditions are held in one visual grammar.
- **Figure 4:** the broadest domain-transfer object, aligning six post-intervention RL trajectories with a visible midpoint event.
- **Figure 5:** the key mechanism/ablation bridge from Newton–Schulz iterations to SFE/DfI/curvature proxies.
- **Table 1 and Figure 6:** together they bound practical reset cost and iteration dynamics, which are otherwise difficult to recover from the main curves.

### Failure patterns

1. Shaded ribbons and Figure 5(b) error bars are not defined as SD, SE, range or another aggregation, despite the paper reporting three or five seeds.
2. Main outcome figures provide curves and approximate bars but no exact endpoint/result tables, denominators or statistical tests; the visual evidence is therefore hard to numerically audit.
3. Eleven-method categorical palettes are not grayscale-safe, and local legends force readers to re-learn colors across domains.
4. Tables 7–10 use a generic `Value` header that hides the parameter identity; search budget, selection criterion and tuning provenance remain in prose.
5. The public repository has matching rendered assets for Figures 2–4 but no plotting scripts, data exports, paper TeX or table generators for exact reproduction; the automatic inventory row consequently understates the partial visual-source evidence.
6. The recurrence coefficients differ between the Algorithm 1 discussion on p. 6 and Appendix B.3 on p. 24, so the coefficient comparison is not fully source-stable; Figure 5 also contains the “Netwon-Schulz” caption typo.
7. The paper's theoretical proxy chain is assumption-bound and does not visually measure the claimed rank/dormancy consequences; the main visuals show DfI/SFE and downstream behavior but not a direct neuron-activity or effective-rank diagnostic.

### One-sentence visual strategy

A restrained ICLR serif layout uses one conceptual geometry figure, rasterized protocol-aligned small multiples and proxy/iteration diagnostics, plus compact appendix configuration tables to move from FIRE's stability–plasticity objective to cross-domain behavior, while leaving uncertainty definitions, exact numeric result surfaces and source-generating code incomplete.
