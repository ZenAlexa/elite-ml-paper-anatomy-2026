## 最终判断

- **最可复用模式**：Use a single two-branch architecture diagram with identical downstream endpoint to expose foveal/peripheral mechanism and its method-interface boundary.；Keep own/random conditions visually stable with hue plus marker/line-style redundancy across feature-analysis panels.；Pair quantitative ablation bars or curves with matched qualitative image cards, and print exact rates or ordered levels directly in the object.；Use a fixed qualitative SAME/DIFFERENT grid across human-fixation and random-fixation conditions so only the manipulated input changes.；For competing similarity measures, combine marginal distributions, a joint scatter, and a few labeled exemplars rather than relying on one metric plot.
- **最高价值对象**：Figure 1: clearest method-interface and training/inference conditioning map.；Figure 4: strongest visual bridge from neural hierarchy proxies to same-judgment outcome.；Figure 13: compact stream ablation with exact fooling rates plus qualitative checks.；Figure 14: broadest stream-specific mechanism replication across seven feature axes.；Figure 12: most persuasive triangulation of semantic versus pixel similarity.
- **主要失败模式**：All quantitative figures omit a clear definition of error bars, sample denominator, aggregation unit, and random seed.；Many raster composites use small labels and hue-dependent encodings, weakening grayscale and low-resolution readability.；Qualitative montages show representative examples without selection rules or IDs, so they cannot substitute for population-level estimates.；Heterogeneous metric directions (SiLog/DreamSim versus mIoU/CLIP) and categorical ablation levels are visually connected without a complete direction/scale key.；The PDF contains no Table object and therefore lacks a compact, auditable tabular summary of participant counts, rates, and uncertainty.
- **一句话视觉策略**：The paper visualizes a dual-stream foveal/peripheral generator, makes its behavioral judgments concrete with matched image grids, then layers correlated feature panels and appendix ablations to argue that semantic and structured scene information—not pixel distance alone—supports metameric perception.
