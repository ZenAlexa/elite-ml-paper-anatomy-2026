## Final judgment

### most_reusable_patterns
- A teaser contract that places arbitrary inputs, the learned depth/ray representation, fused output, and one compact quantitative summary on one page.
- A stable dataset→metric header grammar reused across Tables 1, 2, 5, 6 and 14, with captions documenting metric exceptions and ranking emphasis.
- Reference/input columns followed by identically ordered method columns and a ground-truth column for geometry and NVS qualitative grids.
- A direct method-to-ablation bridge: introduce the module visually, quantify it in a row-controlled table, then show a matched qualitative intervention.
- A teacher-supervision sequence that makes data quality visible (Figure 7), reports design/data choices (Tables 11–13), and shows before/after structure (Figure 8).

### highest_value_objects
- Figure 1 — paper-level input/representation/output and headline benchmark teaser.
- Figure 2 — compact single-transformer/depth-ray/camera-token interface.
- Tables 1–2 — broad pose and reconstruction comparisons under explicit dataset/condition headers.
- Tables 5–6 and Table 14 — controlled representation, architecture, teacher and pose-conditioning ablations.
- Table 4 paired with Figure 9 — quantitative and qualitative NVS evidence across in/out-of-domain datasets.

### failure_patterns
- Nearly all metrics are single point estimates without uncertainty, scene-level spread, or repeat-run information.
- Qualitative depth/point/NVS grids omit numeric scales, colorbars, selection rules, and error localization, so visual superiority is hard to reproduce quantitatively.
- Color-only top-three cells and trajectory/model colors are weak in grayscale and are not consistently backed by redundant encodings.
- Dense wide tables mix metrics, units and precision; Table 14’s ground-truth-pose rows omit cells and require prose to explain the evaluation change.
