## Paper-level visual style

- **Main objects:** 7 figures (Figures 1–7); **main tables:** 0.
- **Appendix objects:** 6 figures (Figures 8–13); **appendix tables:** 0.
- **Dominant figure typography:** the PDF embeds Nimbus Roman/Computer Modern for captions and equations, and imported or plotted objects expose Arial, DejaVu Sans, Calibri, Cambria Math, and Times New Roman. Figure-internal labels are typically estimated at 3.5–8 pt, with Figure 1’s schematic labels reaching about 13 pt. Weight is regular/bold with italic mathematical emphasis; the mixed PDF-object/rendered estimate has high confidence for family presence and medium confidence for point sizes.
- **Palette strategy:** the paper uses local two-colour blue/red line grammars for failure, mechanism, and stabilization curves; red/blue diverging maps for matrices; blue/orange token-specific curves in the Llama panels; a blue-to-yellow sequential scale for spectral norms; and a multi-colour token-difference grid. There is no global colour dictionary, and color alone carries several important distinctions.
- **Vector/raster mix:** most plots, heatmap cells, axes, and labels are vector PDF paths/text. Figure 1 contains small embedded raster/icon objects; Figure 10 is composed of two embedded raster screenshots; the heatmap colour bars in Figures 4, 8, 11, and 12 expose indexed raster strips while the surrounding matrix/plot geometry is vector-like. The overall document is therefore mixed rather than uniformly vector.
- **Consistency:** the main analytical plots share compact Matplotlib-like grids, small sans-serif labels, and blue/red series. Appendix Figures 8–13 become much denser, use imported Arial/DejaVu objects, and place long labels or token strings at near-minimum readable size. Caption typography remains the paper’s serif body style, but figure typography is not fully unified.

## Final judgment

- **Most reusable patterns:**
  1. Reverse-map a long numerical mechanism into failure, localization, root cause, and intervention nodes (Figure 1).
  2. Use a fixed replay and precision/module substitutions before making a causal claim (Figures 2–3 and the surrounding text).
  3. Pair source matrices with derived rank-1 maps, cumulative coefficients, local sign traces, and a zoomed arithmetic event (Figures 4–6).
  4. Validate a minimal intervention with common axes across optimizer/model small multiples (Figure 7), while placing implementation edge cases in an appendix algorithm.
- **Highest-value objects:** Figure 1 for navigation; Figure 4 for the low-rank mechanism; Figure 5 for connecting δ to gradient/output signs; Figure 6 for the `P̄V` event; Figure 7 for the practical stabilization decision. Figure 11 is the most useful appendix context for global layer/time localization, while Figure 10 is useful only as external failure provenance.
- **Failure patterns:** very short/non-self-contained captions for Figures 5, 6, 11, and 12; pervasive color-only variable identity; dual y-axes without explicit scale/lag guidance; selected single trajectories/slices presented without uncertainty or population context; appendix heatmaps and token grids whose labels are too small; screenshots with inconsistent UI, scales, and typography; no visual cost/overhead object for the “minimal” intervention.
- **One-sentence visual strategy:** the paper uses a reverse causal map plus progressively local diagnostics—from replayed loss failure through low-rank/sign/rounding evidence to a common-axis stabilization test—while moving dense cross-model and token-level context to an appendix without providing editable plotting sources.
