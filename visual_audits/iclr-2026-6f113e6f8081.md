## Cross-object system

- **visual_narrative:** The visual story moves from Fig. 1’s raw-text batch → retriever similarity → in-batch attention → NTP/backprop pipeline, to Fig. 2’s exact architectural/mask insertion, then to Table 1 and Fig. 3’s retrieval comparisons. Fig. 4/5 test batch-size and LM-size factors. Appendix Fig. 6 gives a training-dynamics example, Fig. 7 gives a 16-chunk corpus batch, Tables 3–6 define benchmark/resources, Tables 7–10 expand per-task and matched controls, and Tables 11–19 cover scaling, mixed/OOD domains, and LM preservation.
- **caption_system:** Captions use a Figure/Table label followed by setup and metric/encoding definitions. Fig. 4/5 and Table 1 state headline findings; Fig. 3 states benchmark conclusions and appendix pointers. Table 2 is notably non-self-contained, only saying “Revela vs. Contriever Performance,” while glossary/resource captions prioritize identity over statistical definitions. No object reports uncertainty, error bars, intervals, or failure counts.
- **table_header_system:** Result tables put Dataset/Task/Model at the left and model size or benchmark metrics in one or two header levels. Tables 1, 7, 8 use wide model-family headers; Tables 11–14 use nested three-way factor groups; Tables 15–19 use compact model-size columns. Booktabs-style top/mid/bottom rules and bold row winners are consistent; gray fill is reserved for supervised models in Table 1.
- **method_result_ablation_link:** The method vocabulary—Revela, retriever size, LM size, batch size, Wikipedia/code/Fineweb-edu corpus, CoIR/BRIGHT/BEIR, Contriever, and REPLUG—recurs across figures, tables, captions, and appendix sections. Main Table 1/Fig. 3 establish performance, Table 2 and Tables 9–10 isolate controls, Fig. 4/5 and Tables 11–18 expose scaling/domain factors, while Table 19 checks LM capacity preservation.
- **main_appendix_link:** Main objects are intentionally short: Table 1 and Fig. 3 use task means; Table 2 controls the backbone. Appendix A/B/C restore the omitted task rows, benchmark taxonomies, URLs/licenses, training example, Contriever setup, batch/LM-size interactions, mixed/OOD training, and LM results. The paper’s headline claims are readable in the main text but per-task heterogeneity and reproduction details depend on these appendix tables.
- **typography_consistency:** PDF body/captions/tables use NimbusRomanNo9L and Computer Modern math with bold/italic variants; chart text uses DejaVu Sans-like sans-serif glyphs, and Figure 1 includes Georgia/Arial-like embedded labels. Table type is compact (approximately 6.5–8 pt body/header), while charts use small sans-serif ticks and direct labels. Colored hyperlink annotation boxes create additional non-semantic visual noise.
- **color_consistency:** Blue/orange/green are reused for factor levels in Figures 4–5, while Figure 3 mixes family/opacity semantics and Figures 1/6/7 use local semantic colors. Bold is the dominant redundant cue in tables; Figure 1/6/7 add underline/italic, but Figures 3–5 rely mostly on hue/opacity. The palette is not uniformly grayscale safe and the PDF link overlays should be ignored when inferring semantic colors.

## Final judgment

- **Most reusable patterns:**
  - Draw the training signal as a concrete batch-to-retriever-to-attention-to-loss pipeline before introducing equations (Fig. 1 and Fig. 2).
  - Keep headline benchmark means in a paired bar figure, then publish per-task matrices with identical model/metric ordering in the appendix (Fig. 3 and Tables 7–8).
  - Represent scaling ablations with nested factor headers and matched CoIR/BEIR tables so batch size, retriever size, LM size, and domain composition remain separable (Fig. 4/5 and Tables 11–18).
  - Expose resource URLs/licenses and an illustrative batch/training snapshot as separate reproduction objects rather than mixing them into performance tables (Fig. 6/7 and Tables 3–6).
- **Highest-value objects:**
  - Fig. 1 (p.2) and Fig. 2 (p.4): the method interface and attention-mask insertion are the strongest mechanism visuals.
  - Table 1 (p.7) and Fig. 3 (p.7): the main retrieval comparison links CoIR, BRIGHT, BEIR, baseline family, and efficiency context.
  - Table 2 (p.8) plus Tables 9–10 (p.20): matched Contriever controls provide the clearest causal comparison within the otherwise broad leaderboard.
  - Tables 11–18 (pp.20–23): per-task scaling, mixed-domain, and OOD matrices prevent the broader generalization narrative from resting on means alone.
- **Failure patterns:**
  - The public repository has only rendered PNG counterparts; no editable plotting/table source exists for Figures 2/4–7 or Tables 2–19, and the BRIGHT/BEIR asset has revised values relative to the PDF.
  - Mean-only headline charts and bold-only tables provide no run variance, confidence interval, significance, failure count, or cost accounting despite efficiency/generalization claims.
  - Color and opacity are primary encodings in the bar figures; grayscale and small-screen readability are weak, while model labels are rotated or abbreviated.
  - Appendix tables are complete but dense, and many captions (especially Table 2) do not define metrics, aggregation, resource versions, or comparison conditions without surrounding prose.
- **One-sentence visual strategy:** Translate raw-text co-training into a visible attention pipeline, use mean benchmark bars for the headline, and move every task-level, scaling, domain-shift, and reproduction detail into matched booktabs appendix matrices.
